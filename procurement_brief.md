# Request for Proposal (RFP)
## Design, Tooling, Sampling and Supply of Molded TPE Floor Mats

**RFP reference:** PMW-P1-INT-2026-01  
**Issue date:** 18 October 2026  
**Issuing buyer:** Pravaah Mobility Works (fictional)  
**Vehicle program:** Pravaah P1 small hatchback (fictional)  
**Currency:** Indian rupees (INR)  
**Response type:** Technical and commercial proposal

> **Training scenario only.** The buyer, vehicle, suppliers, company histories, quotations, prices, capacities and all commercial terms in this package are invented. This RFP is not an actual sourcing event, market benchmark, safety approval or purchase commitment. Supplier PDFs are synthetic responses for evaluator practice.

## 1. Invitation and purpose

Pravaah Mobility Works invites capable suppliers to propose the engineering, tooling, validation, pilot production and series supply of a four-piece molded thermoplastic elastomer (TPE) floor-mat set for the fictional P1 cabin. This is an introductory automotive sourcing exercise: the product has a clear physical interface, a manageable tooling decision and measurable fitment, quality, cost and delivery factors.

The buyer seeks a supplier able to convert a controlled cabin template and retention samples into a well-fitting product, establish repeatable production controls, support a gated pilot, and quote transparent prices. Respondents should identify assumptions and deviations directly. A low price alone will not establish best value; proposals will be reviewed for evidence, total cost, execution risk and support.

This package is structured as an RFP for comparable responses. The selected respondent in a real process would proceed only after due diligence, negotiation, written nomination and a separate purchase order or contract. No forecast in this document constitutes a guaranteed purchase.

### 1.1 Program goals

- Fit all four passenger-compartment floor positions without obstructing pedals, seat movement or approved retention features.
- Use a durable, cleanable material and a surface/edge design suitable for the intended cabin application.
- Establish a documented design freeze, approved reference sample and first-article acceptance before pilot release.
- Make tooling ownership, maintenance, price components, tax, freight, warranty and change handling explicit.
- Compare supplier responses consistently using the evaluation model in Section 8.

### 1.2 Procurement roles and communication

For this exercise, the buyer's procurement lead coordinates clarification and commercial comparison; the vehicle engineering contact controls fitment inputs and design approval; the quality contact approves the control plan, first article and pilot evidence; and the receiving site confirms packaging and delivery requirements. Respondents should nominate equivalent commercial, engineering and quality contacts in their submissions. In a live process, all clarifications would be issued through one controlled channel so every respondent receives the same information.

## 2. Scope of supply

The requested scope includes, at minimum, design/input review, proposed material and construction, tooling concept and manufacture, fitment samples, fitment correction proposal, first-article documentation, pilot production, production readiness information, series unit pricing, packaging, delivery plan and warranty/support. Respondents must show included, excluded and optional items separately. Identify any external process or subcontractor and the control applied to it.

The planning sequence is **20 fitment/sample sets**, followed by a **200-set pilot** after written sample approval, followed by possible series orders. The indicative annual planning volume is **10,000 sets**. The forecast is non-binding and may change; only released purchase orders and agreed call-offs are firm. Quote the sample lot and pilot lot separately if their pricing differs from series supply.

## 3. Product and performance requirements

The base item is one set containing a driver mat, front passenger mat and two rear mats. Each set must be identified as a four-piece set and packed to prevent deformation, abrasion or contamination in transit.

| Attribute | Buyer requirement / response requested |
|---|---|
| Material | Molded, cleanable TPE. State exact proposed grade, supplier, hardness, recycled-content status if applicable, and material evidence available. Alternatives require clear deviation approval. |
| Colour and appearance | Black/charcoal appearance with consistent texture. State colour control and how visual limits will be agreed. |
| Thickness | Nominal body thickness around 3.0 mm. State actual nominal, tolerance and measurement points. |
| Coverage and fit | Four pieces shaped from buyer-controlled floor template and retention-point samples. Final geometry is subject to a signed controlled drawing and physical fit approval. |
| Anti-slip | Textured underside or molded ribs suitable for keeping the mat positioned under intended use. Describe the feature and validation approach. |
| Perimeter | Raised edge target 8–12 mm. State nominal and tolerance; explain how edge continuity is checked. |
| Driver heel area | Reinforced wear area integrated or securely attached. State dimensions, construction and inspection. |
| Retention | Compatible with buyer-supplied retention points. Describe clip/retention design; do not substitute without written approval. |
| Safety and interface | No sharp edges, loose parts or interference with accelerator, brake or clutch operation, seat movement, adjustment or approved vehicle features. Demonstrate at fitment approval. |
| Odour and cleanability | Describe internal checks or available test evidence. No numerical limit is implied until agreed in the controlled specification. |

### 3.1 Engineering data and design control

Shortlisted respondents may receive a controlled floor template, vehicle access arrangement and retention samples after confidentiality and access steps are agreed. The respondent shall list required input files and samples, identify data gaps, maintain revision control, and submit its proposed drawing and critical-to-fit dimensions for review before tool freeze. Buyer review does not transfer design responsibility or waive supplier obligations. Any deviation must be submitted in a written deviation request with impact, risk and proposed validation; silence is not approval.

### 3.2 Sample and fitment approval

Submit a sample plan covering quantity, material lot identification, tool condition, drawing revision, inspection report and dispatch date. At fitment review the buyer will assess position, edge coverage, clip engagement, movement, pedal clearance, seat travel, visual finish and set completeness. Corrections and included loops must be stated in the quote. Written approval of the signed reference sample is required before pilot production. Any later product or process change requires advance notification and buyer disposition.

## 4. Manufacturing, quality and traceability

Respondents shall describe the process from incoming compound through molding, trimming/finishing, inspection, packing and dispatch. Identify process controls and outsourced activities. The initial quality submission should include a draft process flow, control plan or equivalent inspection plan, first-article report format, traceability label example, nonconformance workflow and corrective-action timing.

At minimum, explain how you will:

1. Verify incoming material identity and link each finished lot to its material batch.
2. Approve the first production piece and compare it to the controlled drawing/reference sample.
3. Check critical dimensions, edge height, retention locations, visual defects and four-piece set completeness.
4. Identify production date, shift/line, tool/cavity where relevant, quantity and inspection status on the lot record.
5. Segregate suspect or nonconforming product, notify the buyer, contain affected stock and document disposition.
6. Preserve records and retained samples for a proposed period; state that period and retrieval time.

State inspection frequency and sample size rather than using terms such as “regular checks.” Submit certificates or test reports that exist and identify their scope/date. Do not claim automotive certification or independent testing unless a valid document is available for buyer review. The buyer may request a quality audit or additional verification before nomination or during launch, subject to agreed arrangements.

## 5. Tooling, capacity and delivery

Provide a tooling concept, estimated tool life, maintenance interval, storage arrangement, repair responsibility, ownership/identification method and transfer conditions. State whether the price covers prototype and production tooling or one tool only. Identify the included design changes and correction loops. Tool ownership and access must be written into the commercial agreement; the buyer expects buyer-funded dedicated tooling to be identifiable and recoverable subject to payment and agreed obligations.

The supplier shall state monthly available capacity for this part, existing constraints, planned production location, material lead assumptions, minimum order quantity, standard batch size and achievable dispatch cadence. Provide milestone dates relative to receipt of complete approved data and tooling purchase order: design review, tool completion, first sample, correction, sample approval, pilot start, pilot completion and series readiness. Include a recovery plan for tool delay, sample failure, capacity shortfall or material interruption.

Delivery destination for comparison is the buyer's Pune-area receiving plant. State dispatch point, packaging units per carton/pallet, estimated freight per set or shipment, transit assumptions, delivery term, insurance responsibility and any minimum quantity required for quoted freight. Final delivery term and named site must be agreed before award.

## 6. Commercial response and contract assumptions

Provide prices in INR and identify the quote validity. Separate one-time engineering/NRE, prototype tool, production tool, sample sets, pilot set price, series price per four-piece set, packaging, freight, insurance, duties if any and GST. State tax basis without assuming that tax is included. Provide any volume price breaks and the volume bands that trigger them. Identify raw-material indexation or other escalation formula, review interval and evidence needed to invoke it. No escalation is accepted unless negotiated and written into the order.

State payment milestones for tooling, samples, pilot and series deliveries; credit period; invoicing trigger; payment currency; quote validity; cancellation exposure; and any minimum order commitment. List exclusions explicitly. Quote should remain comparable if an item is open: label it “not included / to be quoted,” not zero. State whether freight is actual, fixed, included above a threshold or excluded.

### 6.1 Warranty and support

State warranty duration and when it begins, covered defects, claim submission evidence, acknowledgement time, containment response, root-cause/corrective-action timing, replacement or credit timing, return-material handling and freight responsibility. Name launch support roles and escalation contact. Warranty terms are subject to final contract and must not be inferred from general marketing statements.

### 6.2 Confidentiality and intellectual property

Any drawings, vehicle data, templates or samples supplied by the buyer remain controlled buyer information and shall be used only for this response and awarded work, subject to an agreed confidentiality instrument. Respondents should state ownership of pre-existing process know-how and proposed product/tool design. Rights, tooling transfer and data retention must be agreed in the final contract. This paragraph is a proposal requirement, not a substitute for a signed legal agreement.

## 8. Evaluation methodology

Only submitted evidence and documented clarifications should be scored. Each criterion receives a 0–10 evidence score. The weighted points are calculated as **criterion weight × score ÷ 10** and summed to 100 possible points. The result is a structured comparison aid, not an automatic award. The panel may seek clarification, verify claims and apply pass/fail gates for safety interface, sample acceptance, legal qualification or other mandatory requirements.

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence considered |
|---|---:|---|
| Product and tooling capability | 25% | Material/process understanding, fitment plan, retention, tooling concept, correction method and production readiness |
| Quality and compliance | 25% | Material evidence, first article, inspection controls, traceability, nonconformance containment and records |
| Commercial value | 20% | Normalized set price plus tooling/sample/freight/tax clarity, payment terms, assumptions and total cost transparency |
| Delivery and capacity | 20% | Milestones, sample/pilot schedule, capacity support, delivery cadence and recovery plan |
| Warranty and supplier support | 10% | Warranty coverage, claim response, containment/replacement commitment and launch support |

For consistent scoring, a 0 indicates no usable evidence or an unacceptable response; 1–3 indicates major gaps/high risk; 4–6 indicates partial or moderate evidence; 7–8 indicates clear, supported evidence with manageable gaps; 9–10 indicates complete, specific and verifiable evidence with low residual risk. Evaluators should record a short evidence note and any uncertainty for each score. Conflicts of interest should be disclosed and managed under the buyer's applicable process.
